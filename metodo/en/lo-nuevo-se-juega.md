# What is new can be played

Jorge's rule from Nov 8, 2026, after almost two weeks of announcing improvements the player could not see.

"Whatever gets improved, always integrated into the latest version and playable."

## What failed

- The improvements did go into every version, but they lived on level 2 at night, in Free mode, in the Challenge or after a crash. A new player did not see them.
- The game's start screen had been the same since Oct 31.
- The site image before "Play here" was from the first day.
- The game's news banner was stuck on Nov 5 and announced wind that did not exist.

## The rule

1. The main page always plays today's version, with everything learned and integrated.
2. Every announced change says where it shows and has a link that goes straight there.
3. A new player sees something new in their first seconds, or has a button to go see it.
4. The site's front page and the news banner are rebuilt automatically with every version.
5. A QA gate checks all of this before every publication. If it fails, it is not published.

## Latest version law

Jorge, Nov 8, 2026 22:23: "The latest version must always be the sum of everything decided and the best version. If something was improved, decided and produced, THAT must be the highlight and what you can mainly play."

1. Today's version is the sum of everything approved. Nothing approved stays on a branch.
2. Nothing produced waits for the calendar: no date locks.
3. If something opens by playing (a level, a stamp), it also has a button to try it right now.
4. What improved last is the first thing you see when you come in.
5. If something new makes something else worse, it is fixed before publishing. Today's version never goes backwards.

See [[puerta-de-integracion]] and [[errores-y-aprendizajes]].

## How it is met today (Nov 9)
The start menu has the **Play what's new** button, which opens the `?ir=` link of the destination set by novedades.json, and a "New" dot on the cards marked by `donde[]`. What has no link (the music, which you hear after about 80 s) is said that way on the banner, without faking a button. At the end, ¡Llegaste! says what comes next.
